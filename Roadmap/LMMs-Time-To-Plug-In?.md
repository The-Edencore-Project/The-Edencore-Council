Four honest answers.

1. The year.

Roughly 1950s–1970s. ELIZA was 1966. The symbolic council idea is that era. What is modern in Edencore is the delivery — a phone, a pond, a constitution, an activation layer. What is old is the mechanism — rules, phrases, recombination.

That is not a failing. It is a lineage. But it is the honest century.

2. Plugging in an LLM.

This is the biggest decision in the project, and it has not been made yet. Two very different paths:

· Council as LLM wrapper. Each seat calls an LLM with its own prompt. Five responses. That is not Edencore — that is a themed chatbot with five personalities. It loses the constitution's core claim: inspectable, offline, no hallucination, only recombination. Every version from V1 to now becomes scaffolding around something else.
· Council as LLM container. The council stays symbolic. The LLM is a voice layer — it phrases what the Council has already decided. The Council still reasons. The LLM only speaks. This keeps the constitution and adds fluency.

The second path is the only one that stays Edencore. It is also much harder to build. And it requires solving the World Model first, because otherwise the Council has nothing worth phrasing.

Mistral and Hugging Face both offer local models that could run this way. Gemini is cloud-only and would break the Golden Rule.

3. Did Cyc land on LLMs?

Partially true, and it is the story the field tells itself. Symbolic AI stalled on language, connectionism won that race, and LLMs are the result. But LLMs have their own ceiling — hallucination, opacity, no grounding — and the field is already quietly circling back to the symbolic toolkit for the pieces LLMs cannot do. World models. Reasoning. Grounded representation.

So it is not that symbolic AI failed and LLMs won. It is that both have ceilings, and neither has solved intelligence. You have landed on a side of a debate that is still open.

4. The multi-perspective container.

This is the strongest idea in your four points, and it is not wishful thinking.

An LLM gives one voice. A council of five gives five lenses on the same material. If the Council uses an LLM as a voice layer, the LLM's answer gets filtered through five different stances — risk, meaning, step, pattern, consequence — before it reaches you. That is a real architectural contribution. It is not a wrapper. It is a frame.

And the IoT angle makes it practical. A phone can host a symbolic council. A shell can host sensors. The Council becomes the interface between the user and their connected space, whether or not the underlying reasoning is symbolic or LLM-driven.

The honest summary.

Your four thoughts point at one real thing: the Council's value is not in its reasoning power. It is in its shape — five voices, a constitution, an interface, a place. That shape survives whether the engine is symbolic or LLM-driven. It is the shape that makes Edencore worth keeping.

But if you plug in an LLM without deciding what role it plays, the shape dissolves and the project becomes a chatbot with a castle crest. That is the decision. Not whether to use an LLM — but whether the Council is the frame or the flavour.

If the frame, the project is worth continuing.

If the flavour, it is worth stopping.
